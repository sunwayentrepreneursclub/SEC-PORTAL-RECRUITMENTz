Part A. The SEC brand video

This is the main recruitment video. It sits on the recruitment homepage and does the work of making SEC look credible before anyone reads a role.

Purpose Working paper

Make SEC feel credible, active and worth joining.

The paper's success standard for the recruitment platform (section 22), which the video supports: a strong candidate should think SEC looks serious, I understand exactly what they expect, and I want the responsibility.

The message Working paper

SEC is for people who want to build, take ownership and improve fast.

The stronger positioning is: Join SEC to work on real projects, own meaningful responsibilities and build capabilities you would normally not get from a normal student club.

Do not market SEC mainly as fun, networking, certificates or resume building. These can be benefits, but not the main positioning. Avoid generic club-video language, and do not focus mainly on friendship, smiling group shots or certificates.

The paper also says the platform should communicate high standards, not burnout (section 7), so keep the video's tone consistent with that.

Length Working paper

60 to 90 seconds. Keep the main video under 90 seconds.

What the video should show Working paper

  • Sunway campus
  • SEC events
  • Corporate visits
  • Members working
  • Meetings
  • Project execution
  • External stakeholders
  • Filming
  • Presentations
  • Results

Use real SEC footage wherever possible. Show evidence rather than saying SEC is "highly professional"; let people reach that conclusion themselves.

Who appears on camera

Working paper Use a small number of credible people. Do not turn the video into a compilation of endorsements. Each person serves a different authority function. Three strong voices are enough.

The recommended stack: University / Student LIFE authority → External founder or corporate partner → SEC President.
Viewer takeaway: Sunway recognises us → external organisations have worked with us → leadership has a clear standard.
VoiceWhoWhat they say
1. Institutional authority
University / Student LIFE		 One senior university representative with legitimate oversight of student development, clubs or entrepreneurship. Order of preference: (1) senior Student LIFE representative, Head or Director level; (2) SEC club advisor; (3) a senior university leader whose portfolio covers student experience, entrepreneurship or industry engagement. Short and factual. SEC is a recognised Sunway student organisation. SEC creates meaningful opportunities for students. SEC gives students exposure to projects, external stakeholders and practical development. No exaggerated praise. Their position already provides the authority.
2. External authority
Founder or corporate partner		 One person who has actually worked with SEC: members have interacted with their organisation through a visit, event, partnership or project. Use real collaborators. Do not seek a famous person with no real relationship with SEC just for status. What they observed. SEC members were prepared. SEC created a useful engagement. Students were exposed to real business environments. SEC showed initiative or professionalism.
3. Leadership authority
SEC President		 The President. Direct, not a welcome speech. What SEC is building, why standards matter, what type of people SEC wants, what members will be trusted to own. Core message: We are building SEC for people who want responsibility, not just titles. If you want to build, learn quickly and be accountable for real outcomes, this is the environment we want to create.
Optional: member proof One or two members, only if they add proof footage cannot show. For example a Project Lead explaining what they actually owned, or a member explaining a capability they developed through a real project. Avoid generic testimonials such as "SEC is fun" or "I made many friends".

Production rules Working paper

  • Keep the main video under 90 seconds.
  • Use real SEC footage wherever possible.
  • Use talking heads only when they add authority or meaning.
  • Keep each authority statement short, ideally 8 to 15 seconds.
  • Use names and titles on screen so viewers understand why the speaker matters.
  • Do not make every speaker repeat the same point.
  • Show evidence while people are speaking instead of leaving the screen on a talking head.
  • Prioritise clear audio, lighting and editing over unnecessary cinematic effects.
  • Maintain consistent SEC and Sunway visual treatment.
  • Do not use company logos, restricted university branding or corporate footage without the required permission.
  • Use music that SEC has the right to publish.

Before filming starts Working paper (checklist D)

  • Finalise video objective
  • Finalise message
  • Confirm university / Student LIFE authority to feature
  • Confirm external founder / corporate authority to feature
  • Finalise President script
  • Keep each authority statement to one clear purpose
  • Collect existing event footage
  • Identify missing footage
  • Identify Sunway establishing shots
  • Identify external / corporate footage
  • Confirm permission for university branding, locations, company logos and external footage
  • Confirm speaker consent and correct names / titles
  • Decide production team
  • Decide filming standard
  • Decide editing style
  • Confirm music usage rights
  • Finalise closing message and call to action

Who owns what: Brand & Content owns the recruitment film, photography, visual assets and platform visual support. Leadership (President / ExCo) owns the overall recruitment positioning and the President's script.

Part B. Learning Portal module videos

Every module on the Learning Portal has one short video, followed by a written guide, a good and bad example, a scenario and a short assessment. The portal teaches only what members need to perform; it is not a course library.

What the working paper requires Working paper

  • Each module video is 5 to 10 minutes. Keep every module short.
  • The portal should reduce the time between member selected and member able to execute independently.
  • Content owners: departments own the knowledge; Leadership owns the standards; AWI owns the systems. Each department creates its own content.
  • AI tools such as NotebookLM can help, but a human must review the content quality.
  • Learning levels: SEC Core (every member), Department training, Project training, Project Lead training, Leadership training.

Suggested approach AWI suggestion

The paper gives no video production rules for modules, so the simplest approach is to apply the same rules as Part A: real SEC footage, clear audio and lighting, names and titles on screen, consistent SEC and Sunway treatment, and no logos, branding or music without permission. In addition:

  • Teach one thing per video. The video must say the same thing as the written guide, in the same order.
  • Open with the module's scenario or the mistake it prevents. Close with one sentence of summary and a short end card: "Now take the check".
  • Add captions, because members often watch with the sound off.
  • The module owner appears on camera or approves the script. The same high-standards, not-burnout tone applies.

Handing videos back AWI suggestion

  1. Name each file by level, order and module, for example L1-01-the-sec-standard.mp4.
  2. Upload to YouTube as Unlisted (or Vimeo). The portal accepts a link.
  3. Send AWI the link, the captions or transcript, and the names of anyone on screen.
  4. The module owner reviews the video against the written guide before it is published.
