SEC brand video

The main recruitment video. It makes SEC feel credible, active and worth joining before anyone reads a role.

Length60 to 90 seconds. Under 90.
Where it goesRecruitment website, homepage
Send asYouTube link (Unlisted)
YouTube linkPaste the link here

The message the viewer must take away

SEC is for people who want to build, take ownership and improve fast.

Join SEC to work on real projects, own meaningful responsibilities and build capabilities you would normally not get from a normal student club.

What must be in the video

  1. Proof that SEC is real and active. Sunway campus, SEC events, corporate visits, members working, meetings, project execution, external stakeholders, presentations and results.
  2. A university voice. One senior Student LIFE or university representative saying: SEC is a recognised Sunway student organisation, it creates meaningful opportunities for students, and it gives students exposure to projects, external stakeholders and practical development. Facts only, no exaggerated praise. Preferred order: senior Student LIFE representative (Head or Director), then the SEC club advisor, then a senior university leader whose work covers student experience, entrepreneurship or industry engagement.
  3. An external voice. One founder, corporate partner or stakeholder who has really worked with SEC, saying what they saw: members were prepared, the engagement was useful, students saw real business environments, SEC showed initiative or professionalism. Not a famous person with no real link to SEC.
  4. The SEC President. Direct, not a welcome speech: what SEC is building, why standards matter, what type of people SEC wants and what members will be trusted to own. Core message: We are building SEC for people who want responsibility, not just titles. If you want to build, learn quickly and be accountable for real outcomes, this is the environment we want to create.
  5. The closing line. Don't join for the title. Join to become capable. then the call to action: Explore Roles → Apply.

Optional: one or two members, only if they add proof footage cannot show, such as a Project Lead explaining what they actually owned. Three strong voices in total are enough.

Must not be in the video
  • Friendship, smiling group shots or certificates as the main focus.
  • Generic club-video language, or anyone saying "SEC is highly professional". Show the evidence instead.
  • Every speaker repeating the same point. Each voice has a different job.

Before filming, confirm who the university and external speakers are, that they agree to appear, and that you have permission for any logos, locations or footage that are not SEC's own.

The SEC standard

Level 1, SEC Core. Required for every member.

TopicWhat SEC expects from every member, and what members should not expect.
Length5 to 10 minutes. One topic only.
Who speaksThe President or an ExCo member (Leadership owns the standards)
Send asYouTube link (Unlisted)
YouTube linkPaste the link here

By the end the viewer can

Say what SEC expects of them in six areas, name what they should not expect from SEC, and know what to do when a teammate goes quiet on a deadline.

What must be in the video

  1. The situation to open with. You own the sponsor deck for an event next week. A teammate who was meant to supply the budget slide has gone quiet for three days, and your lead has not asked about it. What do you do?
  2. The six standards, each with one real SEC example:
    • Ownership: if it is yours, you carry it to the end.
    • Reliability: people can plan around what you said you would do.
    • Quality: you check your own work before anyone else has to.
    • Learning: you get better quickly and use feedback.
    • Communication: you raise problems early, while they can still be solved.
    • Accountability: you can explain what happened and what you will do next.
  3. What members should not expect: constant instructions, titles without responsibility, guaranteed leadership positions, repeated deadline extensions, no accountability, or lower standards because the club is voluntary.
  4. Good versus bad. Good: you tell your lead on Monday, explain the blocker, propose a new date and ask for help on the one part you cannot solve alone. Bad: you say nothing, hope to catch up, and ask for another extension on the deadline.
  5. The closing point. SEC asks for high standards, not burnout. Members give SEC their time, so SEC owes them an organisation worth that time.

The member is then tested on

So these points must be clear in the video: raise a problem early with a plan; SEC offers responsibility and accountability, not titles or extensions; SEC wants high standards, not burnout; telling your lead early is the communication standard.

How SEC builds people

Level 1, SEC Core. Required for every member.

TopicThe path from selection to leadership, and who owns each part of it.
Length5 to 10 minutes. One topic only.
Who speaksWhoever owns SEC's systems (AWI), with the President on standards if available
Send asYouTube link (Unlisted)
YouTube linkPaste the link here

By the end the viewer can

Describe how SEC takes someone from selected to capable to leader, say who owns standards, knowledge and systems, and know what a module is and how it is completed.

What must be in the video

  1. The situation to open with. A new member's first week. They have been selected. What happens next?
  2. The SEC talent lifecycle: Attract, Select, Train, Deploy, Evaluate, Promote. Recruitment controls who enters. Learning controls how quickly they become capable. Projects give them responsibility. Performance shows who delivers. Leadership development decides who is given more authority.
  3. The learning path:
    • Know SEC: required for every member.
    • Know your role: your department's own training.
    • Know how to execute: project training.
    • Know how to lead: Project Lead and Leadership training, for those heading that way.
  4. Who owns what. Leadership owns standards. Departments own knowledge. AWI owns systems. If a guide is out of date, tell the owner named on the module so it is fixed for everyone.
  5. How a module works. Every module is short: a video, a written guide, a good and a bad example, a scenario and a short assessment. Passing the assessment marks the module complete.

The member is then tested on

So these points must be clear in the video: who owns standards, knowledge and systems; what to do about an out-of-date guide (tell the owner); a module is complete when you pass the assessment.
